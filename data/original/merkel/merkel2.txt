Dear Fellow Citizens,

I extend my warm greetings to you. I am pleased to have the opportunity, on this New Year’s Eve as well, to share with you a few thoughts that are on my mind as we stand on the threshold of a new year.

From the many conversations and encounters I have had this year, I know that many of you are concerned about social cohesion in Germany. It has been a long time since opinions on this issue were so divided. Some even speak of a rift running through our society.

Some say: Germany is a wonderful country in which the values of our Basic Law are lived out. A country that is strong and economically successful. A country in which more people are in employment today than ever before. A country with an open-minded and diverse society, with a strong sense of solidarity, where millions of people volunteer every day to help others—for example in sport, in support of the sick and vulnerable, or in helping refugees.

Others say: There are too many people who do not share in this success. People who cannot keep pace with the speed of our times. People who see their children moving to the big cities and who are left behind in areas where everyday life, from shopping to visiting a doctor, is becoming increasingly difficult. People who are concerned that there is too much crime and violence. People who ask how we can organize and manage immigration into our country.

Both are realities in our country: success and confidence, but also fears and doubts. For me, both are a source of motivation.

For you, dear fellow citizens, have given us politicians the task of addressing the challenges of the future while keeping the needs of all citizens in mind. I feel committed to this responsibility—especially as we work to form a stable government for Germany swiftly in the new year.

For the world will not wait for us. We must now create the conditions that will ensure Germany is doing well 10 or 15 years from now. And Germany is truly doing well when success benefits everyone and improves and enriches our lives.

In this regard, the guiding principle of the Social Market Economy—that economic success and social cohesion are two sides of the same coin—can continue to serve as our compass in the age of digital progress.

On the one hand, this means:

safeguarding existing jobs while also creating entirely new jobs for the future,

providing companies with even greater support for research and development in innovative technologies,

making the state a pioneer in digitalization,

and, above all, preparing ourselves and our children for digital progress through the best possible education and further training.

On the other hand, it also means:

placing families at the center of our policies and easing their financial burden so that they can balance family life and work even better,

ensuring good and dignified care by strengthening the care professions and providing even greater support to people who care for relatives at home,

and—not least—ensuring equivalent living conditions in every region of our country, whether in cities or in rural areas.

And we will have to invest even more in a strong state that upholds the rules of our coexistence and ensures your security—indeed, the security of us all.

For this reason, I would particularly like to thank the police officers who are there for us this evening as well, protecting, for example, the many New Year’s Eve celebrations taking place across the country, as well as the soldiers who serve our country here at home or on missions abroad.

The future of Germany is, in all of this, inseparably linked to the future of Europe. The 27 states of Europe must, more than ever before, be encouraged to stand together as a community. This will be the decisive question of the years ahead.

The question will be whether we Europeans, in a global and digital world, can uphold our values with solidarity and confidence, both at home and abroad; whether we can work in this way for a Europe that is economically successful and just; and whether we can consistently stand up for the protection of our external borders as well as for the security of our citizens.

Germany and France want to work together to make this possible and thereby help prepare Europe for the future.

Dear Fellow Citizens,

The struggle to find the right answers is part of a vibrant democracy. We are, in the best sense of the word, a society of many voices. At the same time, we are united by the values of our Basic Law: respect for the inviolable dignity of every individual and for their fundamental freedoms.

That we become more aware once again of what, at our core, holds us together; that we once again place greater emphasis on what we have in common; that we strive to show greater respect for one another—and respect in the broadest sense: paying attention, truly listening, and showing understanding—these are my wishes for the new year.

And to you and your families, I wish health, strength, confidence, and God’s blessing for the New Year 2018.
