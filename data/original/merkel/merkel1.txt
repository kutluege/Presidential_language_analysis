Dear fellow citizens,

2016 was a year of severe trials. I would like to speak to you about that this evening — but also about why, despite everything, I remain confident about Germany and why I am so deeply convinced of the strengths of our country and its people.

The most serious challenge is, without doubt, Islamist terrorism, which has also had us Germans in its sights for many years. In 2016, it struck us in the heart of our own country: in Würzburg, in Ansbach, and only a few days ago at the Christmas market here at the Kaiser Wilhelm Memorial Church in Berlin.

And — yes — it is especially bitter and repugnant when terrorist attacks are committed by people who supposedly seek protection in our country. People who, precisely for that reason, have experienced the willingness of our country to help them and who now mock that generosity through their actions. Just as they also mock those who genuinely need and deserve our protection.

So what, then, of the confidence I spoke of at the beginning?

Confidence in the midst of deep grief for the dead and the injured?

I believe we were able to feel it here in Berlin and in many other German cities, especially during these difficult days: in the comfort that we can give or receive.

And in a firm determination to confront the terrorists’ world of hatred with our humanity and our solidarity.

By continuing with our lives and our work, we tell the terrorists: You are murderers filled with hatred, but you do not determine how we live or how we want to live. We are free, humane, and open.

And even with the images of bombed-out Aleppo in Syria before our eyes, we can once again say how important and right it was that, during the past year, our country helped those who genuinely need our protection to gain a foothold here among us and to integrate.

All of this is reflected in our democracy, in our rule of law, and in our values.

They are the opposite of the hate-filled world of terrorism, and they will prove stronger than terrorism. Together, we are stronger. Our state is stronger.

Our state does everything it can to guarantee its citizens security in freedom.

This work is never finished, and especially this year we have provided our security authorities with a great deal of additional support. In 2017, as the Federal Government, wherever political or legislative changes are necessary, we will initiate and implement the necessary measures as quickly as possible.

Many people also associate this year, 2016, with the feeling that the world as a whole has come apart at the seams, or that what was long regarded as an achievement is now being called into question. The European Union, for example. Or even parliamentary democracy itself, which supposedly does not care about the interests of citizens but serves only the benefit of a few.

What distorted images.

Yes, Europe is slow. It is laborious. It has to endure profound setbacks such as the withdrawal of a member state. And — yes — Europe should concentrate on those things it can genuinely do better than the nation state.

But no — we Germans should never allow ourselves to be deceived into thinking that a happy future could ever lie in going it alone as a nation.

Where Europe — as in global competition, the protection of our external borders, or migration — is challenged as a whole, it must also find the answer as a whole, no matter how difficult and arduous that may be. And we Germans have every interest in playing a leading role in that process.

The image that some people draw of our parliamentary democracy is also a distortion. Yet it is strong. It enables participation and a voice in decision-making. It accepts — no, it demands — disagreement and criticism. Criticism that is expressed peacefully and with respect for the individual, that seeks solutions and compromises, and does not exclude entire groups.

2017 is also the year of the next federal election. I will work for a political debate in which we will argue passionately about many things, but always as democrats who never forget that it is an honour to serve our democracy and, through it, the people.

Another source of encouragement for me when I think about Germany is our social market economy. It enables us to cope with crises and processes of change better than any other economic system in the world.

Never before have so many people been in employment as they are today. Most of our companies are in good shape. Our economic success gives us the opportunity to strengthen our social welfare system and to help all those who need help. Starting tomorrow, for example, important improvements in long-term care will come into effect.

I also draw encouragement from the enthusiasm and inventive spirit with which German companies and our universities conduct research and development for the future. Whether in new forms of energy or digitalisation, in every field we have the opportunity not merely to be driven by change, but to be among those who discover and shape new paths.

To do that, we need an open view of the world and confidence — in ourselves and in our country.

Solidarity, openness, our democracy, and a strong economy that serves the well-being of all: these are the things that, even at the end of a difficult year, give me confidence in our future here in Germany.

None of these values is simply given to us. We will have to work for each of them in 2017 as well, all of us together, each according to our abilities — and that work will be worthwhile.

I wish you and your families, from the bottom of my heart, a happy New Year, happiness, good health, and God’s blessing.
