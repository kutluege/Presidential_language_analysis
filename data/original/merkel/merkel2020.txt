Dear fellow citizens,

This evening, we stand not only at the beginning of a new year, but also at the beginning of a new decade. I am convinced that we have good reason to be confident that the 2020s, which will begin in just a few hours, can be good years—if we make use of our strengths, if we focus on what unites us, and if we remember what we have achieved together over the past decades.

Next year, Germany will have been reunified in peace and freedom for 30 years. In those 30 years, we have accomplished remarkable things. For example, never before have so many people been in employment as today. Nevertheless, there will still be more to do in the next decade than we thought 30 years ago.

At the same time, we experience every day just how profoundly digital progress is changing our lives in every area, including, of course, our working lives. We must find new answers to this. Because we want everyone to have access to the education they need for this transformation. We want them to continue to have good and secure jobs in the future—and a reliable pension in old age.

To achieve this, more than ever we need the courage to think in new ways, the strength to leave familiar paths behind, the willingness to venture into new territory, and the determination to act more quickly, in the conviction that unfamiliar approaches can succeed—and must succeed, if today’s younger generation and their descendants are still to be able to live well on this Earth.

The warming of our planet is real. It is threatening. It, and the crises arising from global warming, are caused by human beings. Therefore, we must do everything humanly possible to overcome this challenge facing humanity. It is still possible.

This conviction also underpins the climate protection programme that the Federal Government and the Länder adopted a few days ago. I am very well aware that the measures decided upon make some people afraid that they may be overwhelmed by them, while for others they still do not go nearly far enough.

And it is also true that, at the age of 65, I am at a point in my life when I personally will no longer experience all the consequences of climate change that would arise if policymakers failed to act. It is our children and grandchildren who will have to live with the consequences of what we do—or fail to do—today. That is why I am devoting all my strength to ensuring that Germany makes its contribution—ecologically, economically and socially—to bringing climate change under control. The law that has just been adopted provides the necessary framework for doing so—in the literal sense of the word.

In doing this, we can build on what has always made us strong: our ideas, our inventive spirit, our diligence and perseverance, our craftspeople, engineers and skilled workers, our state and voluntary structures, our way of living together in families and associations, and our appreciation for those who, for example, work in care, for and with other people.

And we are guided by the values of our Basic Law: freedom, solidarity and respect for the dignity of every individual, as well as by the principles of the social market economy. They will remain our compass in the next decade as well. This means that even in the digital age, technology must serve people—not the other way around. Human dignity sets the limits, because it is inviolable.

This is the core of our liberal democracy, which must be brought to life anew every day. I thank the women and men who assume political responsibility in our country, especially those at the municipal level. Protecting them—as well as everyone in our country—from hatred, hostility and violence, from racism and antisemitism, is the responsibility of the state, a responsibility to which the Federal Government feels particularly committed.

I thank the many people who work for the good of our community, both professionally and on a voluntary basis: the police officers, firefighters and all those who stand by their fellow citizens in difficult situations. Together, they form the backbone of our democracy.

In recent years, I have often said that in the long term, Germany can only do well if Europe does well too. Because only within the community of the European Union can we uphold our values and interests and safeguard peace, freedom and prosperity.

Europe must make its voice heard more strongly in the world. We will work toward this during Germany’s Presidency of the Council of the European Union in the coming year as well, for example through a summit of all Member States with China and a meeting with the countries of Africa. Cooperation with Africa is also in our own interest. Because only when people have the opportunity to live peaceful and secure lives will flight and migration decrease. Only when we end wars through political solutions will lasting security be achieved.

Our security and prosperity depend to a significant extent on security and economic progress in our neighbourhood as well. That is why this evening I would like to thank our soldiers, our police officers, and our civilian aid workers who are serving far from home.

A new decade lies ahead of us. The 2020s can be good years. Let us surprise ourselves once again with what we are capable of. Change for the better is possible if we embrace new things with openness and determination.

With this in mind, I wish you and your families a healthy, happy and blessed New Year 2020.
