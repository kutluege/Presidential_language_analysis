French women and men, in metropolitan France, in our overseas territories and abroad,

As I extend my best wishes to you, I want to begin by expressing our gratitude to all those among our fellow citizens who, once again this evening, are protecting, caring for others, helping, and ensuring the continuity of the life of the Nation. Thanks to them, we are able to spend these moments of transition from one year to the next with our families and loved ones. And at this moment, I also want to express my particular affection for those who are ill or who will remain alone.

The year 2023 was marked by the continuation of the war in Ukraine, the war now taking place in the Middle East and the bombardments of Gaza; by the terrorist attacks of October 7 in Israel, in which 41 French citizens were killed, and this evening my thoughts are with their families, just as they are with the families of our compatriots who are still being held hostage.

It was also marked by growing geopolitical tensions, by the consequences of climate disruption on our territory, and by acts of terrorism on our soil. By divisions, acts of hatred and, at times, violence on several occasions, which weaken the cohesion of the Nation. Finally, inflation has taken its toll, making everything more difficult: finding housing, getting around, doing the shopping.

There were, of course, happy moments, moments of pride, but I know how present the fear of the return of war, of decline, and of losing control remains.

And yet I am convinced—and this is not false optimism—that in this context of crises, the best can emerge.

That is why I have never ceased to follow the same course: to act with determination and consistency, for today and for tomorrow. For seven years, from the position in which you placed me, I have sought to make France stronger and fairer, to liberate, protect and unite. That is our course, and we are holding to it. And the same was true in 2023.

In 2023, France was undoubtedly one of the Western countries that, despite the circumstances, made the greatest number of decisions and carried out the greatest number of transformations. Some of them, such as the pension reform, were, I know, unpopular. I stand by them because I had committed to them and because they were necessary for the country.

All these past months have therefore been a far cry from the powerlessness that some had predicted for us, and that is a good thing. And I want here to thank especially the Prime Minister and her government.

In 2023, we acted—acted in pursuit of our goal of reindustrialisation and full employment, the first results of which are already visible. Pensions, unemployment insurance, the revival of our green industries, the acceleration of France 2030, the overhaul of vocational high schools, the reform of Pôle emploi and the active solidarity income, and the reform of the European electricity market: all these major transformations were decided upon, voted on, and began to take effect this year.

And this year we continued to create jobs while reducing our greenhouse gas emissions even more rapidly, in accordance with our commitments. This proves that you have risen to the challenge of this collective mobilisation.

Thanks to this economic strengthening, we remain able to finance our social model, to protect ourselves against crises or inflation, but also to invest in strengthening our public services. And that is what we have done.

We invested in our schools and our healthcare system in order to provide better support and better address inequalities of opportunity. We laid the foundations for an unprecedented ecological planning strategy and committed substantial resources to support each and every one of you through this transition.

In 2023, we also passed historic laws for the years ahead in order to protect ourselves. A historic law for our armed forces: over the course of a decade, the budget of our military will have doubled. A historic law for our police officers, gendarmes, magistrates and court clerks, in order to ensure public order and republican unity, including the creation of thousands of positions to better protect you. We will continue to restore authority wherever it is lacking in the face of antisocial behaviour and crime.

On immigration, the law passed in December, together with the agreement reached at the European level, gives us the tools necessary to better uphold the principles of the Republic: that is, both to fight smugglers and illegal immigration, and to better integrate those who are meant to remain on our soil—refugees, students, researchers and workers.

I am, of course, well aware of the impatience—and I would even venture to say that I share it—even though the first changes are already visible. I know that you would like us to do more and to do it faster. And it is to achieve this objective that 2024 must above all be a year of determination. To act, to act again, in the interest of the Nation.

We will be determined to act for schools, childhood and education, in order to restore the level of our pupils, the authority of our teachers, and the strength of our secular and republican education system. After strengthening our economy, strengthening the State and our public services, we must now undertake a civic strengthening of the Nation. France is a culture, a history, a language, universal values that must be learned from the earliest age. By every generation.

We will also be determined to make our Republic stronger by fighting crime, tackling discrimination more firmly, and enabling concrete progress toward equality between women and men, for early childhood, and for our fellow citizens with disabilities. We will be equally determined to intensify the strengthening of our industrial, technological and scientific capabilities. We must do everything we can to achieve our ambition of full employment, continue to innovate, but also attract talent and businesses and produce more in France. Accordingly, next year we will embark on major cutting-edge projects, from nuclear energy to artificial intelligence and transport. So that by 2027, we will be ten years ahead in areas where, in 2017, we were ten years behind.

In 2024, we will have to accelerate our efforts even further and radically simplify the lives of our entrepreneurs, farmers, shopkeepers, craftspeople, elected representatives—particularly our mayors—and many sectors, including housing.

Finally, we will be determined to act so that in 2024 our ecological approach—this distinctively French approach to ecology—and our planning continue to develop as a model and a unique path, allowing us to move away from fossil fuels and gain greater independence and progress. France, which already produces some of the least carbon-intensive electricity in Europe, will completely phase out coal by 2027. And we will pursue this same fight internationally, including through our Pact for People and the Planet.

2024: a year of determination, effectiveness and results. And in the weeks ahead, I will have the opportunity to explain to you how our Nation will meet these challenges.

My dear compatriots, action is not an option. Action is our duty toward future generations. That is why I will tirelessly stand with those who act in the service of the country, and never with those who refuse to tell you the truth in order to justify doing nothing and who, in the end, weaken us. Never with those who put electoral calculations, petty arrangements or their personal interests first.

We must therefore continue strengthening the Nation in the face of the disorder of the world. For strength of character is the virtue required in difficult times.

2024 will also be a year of decisive choices.

We will have to choose a stronger, more sovereign Europe, in light of the legacy of Jacques Delors. A Europe that works for peace in the Middle East and on our own continent, by continuing to support the Ukrainian people and, with them, our security, our freedom and our values. Next June, you will be called upon to decide whether to continue strengthening our European sovereignty in the face of these dangers: whether to stop Russia and support the Ukrainians, or give way to authoritarian powers in Ukraine; whether to continue building Europe or bring it to a halt; whether to continue the ecological and productive transition or turn back; whether to affirm the strength of liberal democracies or give way to the lies that sow chaos.

Finally, 2024 will be a year of French pride. Pride in the thousands of craftsmen, builders and entrepreneurs who have taken part in the magnificent project to rebuild Notre-Dame de Paris, whose spire once again rises toward the sky above a cathedral that will reopen on December 8.

We are a Nation of builders and craftspeople, capable, when united, of resisting and rising again. Yes, in five years, as promised, we will have rebuilt the cathedral and accomplished the impossible. We are that Nation which, when proud of itself, always carries a universal hope.

In 2024, we will also celebrate our History. Eighty years after 1944, we will take pride in our past and in our heroes whose destinies became intertwined. Soldiers of the Allied armies, but also French citizens by birth or by choice, French and foreign members of the Resistance. All fighting so that a France faithful to the spirit of the Enlightenment and to the spirit of our Revolution could be liberated.

2024 will be a year of pride in the French language. After restoring the Château de Villers-Cotterêts and creating there the Cité internationale de la langue française, in a few months we will welcome the French-speaking world there.

2024 will also be a year of French sporting pride, as the Olympic and Paralympic Games will take place here, in France, and therefore, in a sense, at home for all of us, both in metropolitan France and in our overseas territories.

We will be proud of our athletes, our artists, our landscapes, and of this great popular celebration, made possible by thousands of volunteers, who will also build a sporting legacy for our Nation. Through the commitment of us all, beginning tomorrow.

Yes, 2024 will, as you have understood, be a truly French vintage year. Because it is only once every decade that we commemorate our Liberation on such a scale. It is only once a century that we host the Olympic and Paralympic Games. And it is only once in a millennium that we rebuild a cathedral. It is only once in a generation that the destiny of the next is decided, as it undoubtedly is being decided now.

2024: a year of determination, choices, renewal and pride. At its heart, a year of hope. Yes, much of our future will be decided this year. So it is up to us to act together. It is up to us to choose rather than endure, to chart the course rather than follow it.

And our entire History teaches us that the determination of a few can overcome every seeming inevitability. So it is up to us to act—yes, to act together, to continue to move beyond divisions and narrow group interests, because within our people lie the deep reserves of strength needed to meet these challenges.

My dear compatriots, I wish you a very beautiful and very happy year 2024.

So that France may unite, act and shine. May it be worthy of the “indefinable splendour of those who are destined for great undertakings,” because that is who we are.

Long live the Republic. Long live France.
