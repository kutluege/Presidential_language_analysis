French women and men, my dear compatriots,

On this evening of December 31, my thoughts go first and foremost to those who ensure the continuity of the Nation: the security forces, firefighters, volunteers, healthcare workers, workers, and all those who, tonight as always, are on the front line in the face of hardship. Protecting, caring, feeding, helping, providing assistance and solidarity—this is how great nations endure each day, through the dedication of their citizens. On behalf of all of you, my thoughts are with those who are alone, ill, facing hardship or deprivation. I know how difficult the situation is for many of you, confronted with personal suffering and the hardships of life, and I want to express our support and affection.

Thanks to everyone’s commitment, thanks to you, we are holding firm. Yes, our country is holding firm. Strong in its institutions and its public services; strong in its armed forces, which are the most effective in Europe, thanks to the commitment of its soldiers. Strong in our economy, where never before have so many French people been employed, where growth remains steady, and where our inflation is among the lowest in the euro area. Strong in our research, our academic excellence, and also in our diplomatic initiatives, whether those undertaken for peace, the climate, or our oceans.

But beyond all this, no one is blind to the disorder in the world or to our own weaknesses. Our world is becoming harsher every day. War and instability persist in the Near and Middle East, with consequences for our economies, our interests, and sometimes our cohesion. And war continues to rage on European soil, with particular intensity since Russia decided to launch a new aggression against Ukraine nearly four years ago. We are witnessing the return of empires, the questioning of the international order, a world of trade wars, technological competition, and often instability.

I also see our own divisions, our doubts, the growing loneliness within society, the decline in our birth rate, insecurity, and difficulties with purchasing power.

I know all the impatience, and sometimes the anger, that continues to exist in the country, and I share several of those concerns. These urgent matters require answers. From the very first weeks of the year ahead, the Government and Parliament will have to build agreements in order to provide the nation with a budget. This is essential. From the beginning of 2026, we will need to act: to support our farmers in the face of crises and protect them from decisions that could threaten our ability to produce, as well as our food security. To further strengthen our economy by simplifying the rules for our entrepreneurs as well as for our farmers. To persevere in our efforts against insecurity and all forms of antisocial behaviour, and to relentlessly combat drug trafficking in all its forms. To continue regaining control of our French and European borders in the face of illegal immigration; to continue training our teachers and give primary and lower secondary schools the means to better transmit fundamental knowledge; to recognise the expertise of our doctors, both in community care and in hospitals, by simplifying the way they work and allowing them greater freedom to make decisions and organise their activities. To implement concrete and radical decentralisation, developed together with our local elected representatives, in order to simplify the functioning of the country, make savings, and at the same time respond to the legitimate aspirations of Corsica and several overseas territories. For all of this—and I could have listed here every project that lies ahead of us—I know that the Government is hard at work and that the Prime Minister is determined to be of service to the country, and tonight I want to thank them sincerely for that. This will also require everyone’s mobilisation and the efforts of all.

This year, municipal elections will also be held in our towns and communities. And tonight I want once again to express to all our mayors and elected representatives my recognition and gratitude. Over the past few years, they have stood with me through every struggle and every crisis, serving you.

This year must therefore be, and will be, a useful year.

I will pay particular attention to ensuring that several major projects are brought to completion. We will see the first steps of the national service programme for the engagement of our young people, which will strengthen the bond between our armed forces and the nation. We will protect our children and adolescents from social media and screens. And we will finally bring the legislative process concerning the question of a dignified end of life to completion, an issue on which I made a commitment to you in 2022.

At the end of the year, when the time comes, the next campaign for the 2027 presidential election will gradually begin—the first presidential election in ten years in which I will not take part. Nevertheless, until the very last second, I will remain at work, striving every day to live up to the mandate you entrusted to me, and I will do everything possible to ensure that the presidential election takes place as calmly as possible, and in particular free from any foreign interference.

Beyond the work that must be done, I have three wishes for our nation.

First, a wish for unity. Whatever the challenges, our history teaches us that we can overcome anything and face anything if we know how to remain united. Let us therefore never forget that the reasons we have for living together are, every day, stronger than whatever may divide us, stronger than the flood of emergencies, individual incidents, or bad news. Our unity requires us to fight relentlessly against antisemitism, racism, and every form of discrimination. Our unity requires us to recognise that every French woman and every French man has a role to play in meeting the challenges ahead of us, that each and every one of us is necessary and deserves encouragement and recognition. Yes, in our everyday lives, ultimately, I wish for us to show greater kindness and greater humanity.

My second wish is a wish for strength and independence. At a time when the law of the strongest is attempting to impose itself on world affairs and our Europe is under pressure from every side, we must defend our independence and our freedoms.

Our independence requires us to continue investing in our armed forces, our security forces, our public services, and our economy despite financial difficulties. For ten years, I have argued strongly for this independence, and as Europeans we have done a great deal to strengthen it, but now we must accelerate. European defence was for a long time merely a subject of debate; it has now begun to take shape, and in 2026 this process will accelerate. As early as January 6, in Paris, many European states and allies will make concrete commitments to protect Ukraine and secure a just and lasting peace on our European continent. Let us also protect our industrial and agricultural Europe by establishing fair and equitable trade rules in relation to the rest of the world. Let us dare to become a true power, one that embraces a European preference for its jobs and its businesses. Let us build an independent Europe in the space industry, quantum technologies, and artificial intelligence. Our prosperity, both in France and across Europe, depends on it.

My final wish is a wish for hope—hope for ourselves and for our children.

Let us not give up. Let us not give up on progress, which is still possible provided that we build it, work hard, invest for the long term, and recognise that genuine progress does not happen in a single day, but sometimes takes the span of a generation.

Let us not give up on reconciling the climate, biodiversity, growth, and independence. Let us not give up on major scientific discoveries, on our love of science and research, or on economic success.

Let us not give up on the place of reading, beauty, and culture. Let us not give up, day after day, on becoming a nation that is more supportive and more fraternal. Yes, ultimately, I wish for us to resist the spirit of the times, quite simply because we are French. We must stand firm in defending what we cherish: humanity, peace, and freedom. We will succeed. So let us look ahead, and let us look far into the future, as citizens and as a nation.

My dear compatriots in mainland France, in our overseas territories, and living abroad, I wish you a wonderful and very happy year 2026.

Long live the Republic! Long live France!
