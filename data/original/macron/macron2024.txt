My dear compatriots,

Together, this year, we proved that “impossible” is not a French word.

We became the first nation to amend our Constitution in order to guarantee women’s right to voluntarily terminate a pregnancy. We advanced equality between women and men, among other things thanks to the courage of those who dared to speak out.

We continued to build an ecology based on progress, to fight climate change by reducing our emissions, by opening factories, and by continuing to fight for both our present and our future.

We commemorated the bravery of our Resistance fighters and of those who liberated us, 80 years later, from Normandy to Provence.

We remembered those who fell so that France could rise again. Our heroes, our elders, our allies, those who came from every continent to liberate our homeland.

We also experienced, as I promised a year ago, moments of great pride. First of all, this summer we organized Olympic and Paralympic Games that immediately entered history, entered our memories and our hearts, that made a united country come alive, from Saint-Denis to Tahiti, and showed a France full of boldness, flair and extraordinary freedom.

A France that shines through its sporting achievements, its emotions, its generosity, and through a city of Paris shown at its very best.

Our Olympic and Paralympic Games, yes, are and will remain an unforgettable moment in the life of the Nation.

In this month of December 2024, as we had promised, we returned Notre-Dame de Paris to worship and reopened it to the public. Five years after the fire of 2019, the rebuilt Notre-Dame remains a symbol of our French determination.

Thousands of workers, craftsmen, women and men rebuilt the cathedral day after day throughout those five years. It was returned to everyone, and the entire world was able to admire this work.

Tomorrow, let us know how to preserve the best of what we were during this year, 2024. United, determined and in solidarity, and in the face of every major ordeal, in the face of what so many people said was impossible, we succeeded because we stood together.

United, determined and in solidarity, we also stood together in the face of the trials and tragedies of 2024: climate-related disasters, the crisis our farmers are still experiencing today, whom we will continue to protect and support, tensions in several of our territories, and the global economic slowdown.

Together, today we stand alongside the people of Mayotte, whom we are thinking of with emotion and solidarity. And of course, we are and will remain alongside all of our overseas territories, from the Antilles to New Caledonia, which has suffered so greatly in recent months.

We are also confronted with political instability. It is not unique to France; we also see it among our German friends, who have just dissolved their Parliament. But it legitimately concerns us.

I must acknowledge this evening that, for the moment, the dissolution has brought more divisions to the National Assembly than solutions for the French people.

If I decided to dissolve the Assembly, it was to give you back your voice, to restore clarity and to avoid the paralysis that threatened us. But lucidity and humility require us to recognize that, at this point, this decision has produced more instability than calm, and I fully accept my share of responsibility for it.

The current Assembly nevertheless represents the country in all its diversity, and therefore also in its divisions. It is fully legitimate, and in this unprecedented but democratic configuration, it must be capable of forming majorities, as parliaments in the great democracies do, and our government must be able to follow a path of compromise in order to act.

I would like to thank Michel Barnier here, who, after Elisabeth Borne and Gabriel Attal, committed himself with sincerity, and I extend my very best wishes to François Bayrou and his government.

I hope that the year ahead will be one of collective recovery, that it will bring stability and the right compromises needed to make the right decisions in the service of the French people.

Because we cannot afford to wait. The year 2025 must be a year of action, a useful year for you and one that enables you to live better. Purchasing power, employment, security, justice, and support for our elected representatives and our associations: for all of these things, it is necessary to adopt a budget.

We can make life better by agreeing on a few simple matters: making life easier for all those who work hard, improving everyday security, delivering justice more quickly, and enabling every family to have access to the best education for their children, to healthcare, and to basic public services. It is therefore up to us to do this.

The year 2025 must also be a year of unity and responsibility, in order to build a stronger and more independent France in the face of the world’s upheavals.

The wars in Ukraine and the Middle East are not distant conflicts. They concern us directly and threaten our security, our unity and our economy.

Recent events in Syria, the manipulation of elections in Moldova, Georgia and Romania, and terrorist attacks in Europe and in our neighboring regions show just how much our security and the proper functioning of our democracies can never be taken for granted.

That is why Europe can no longer delegate its security and defense to other powers. In 2025, France must continue investing in its military rearmament in order to guarantee our sovereignty, the protection of our interests, and the security of our fellow citizens. Europe, for its part, must accelerate its efforts to take control of its own defense, security and borders. This is what I have been fighting for for so many years, what we have begun to build with our European partners, and what we will continue to pursue.

We must clearly recognize that the world is moving faster and challenging many of our certainties. What we once took for granted can no longer be taken for granted.

There can be no prosperity without security, and France, through its diplomatic and military power, still has a role to play in this respect.

If our children are to live better than we do, the technologies and companies that will shape the world of tomorrow, our future and our growth, must also be developed in France and in Europe: artificial intelligence, the revolutions in quantum technologies, energy and biology, to name only a few of these fields.

Europeans must put an end to their naivety. We must say no to trade rules dictated by others that we are the only ones still respecting, and say no to everything that makes us dependent on others without anything in return and without preparing for our future.

On the contrary, what we need is a European awakening: a scientific, intellectual, technological and industrial awakening, as well as an agricultural, energy and ecological awakening. To achieve this, we must move faster, make our decisions more rapidly and more decisively as Europeans, simplify our rules for our fellow citizens as well as for our businesses, and invest more.

This requires a France that continues to be attractive, that works and innovates more, that continues to create jobs and that ensures its growth while keeping its public finances under control. I will see to it.

For seven years, we have managed to withstand the worst crises while reducing unemployment, reindustrializing the country and attracting innovations from all over the world.

Once again this year, we demonstrated our ability to maintain our standing in sectors of the future, with, for example, the launch of Ariane 6 and the connection of the new Flamanville nuclear reactor to the grid. This is where our independence is being built, and we must remain strong and credible at the heart of a Europe that must accelerate.

It is therefore up to all of us, collectively, to act, because 2025 will demand boldness and decisiveness.

My dear compatriots, great nations are those that, in moments of crisis and doubt, know how to look far ahead, rise above the controversies of everyday life, build the future and stay one step ahead.

This is where we are today. That is why, in 2025, we will stay the course.

We are now a quarter of a century into this century. Some of the promises of the year 2000 have been fulfilled, but so many disruptions and imbalances have also emerged. So, for the quarter-century ahead, I want us to be able to decide and act, with 2050 in our sights.

We will have choices to make for our economy, our democracy, our security and our children. Yes, the hope, prosperity and peace of the quarter-century ahead depend on the choices we make today.

That is why, in 2025, we will continue to make decisions, and I will also ask you to decide on some of these decisive issues. Because each and every one of you will have a role to play. Each and every one of you will be necessary if we are to succeed in the project I have just briefly outlined before you. Yes, just as with the great undertakings that have shaped our history, and again during the year that has just passed, each and every one of you is necessary to build an even greater Nation and Republic.

That is why, for 2025, I wish for all of us to remain united, determined and fraternal.

I wish you and your loved ones a wonderful and very happy year 2025.

Long live the Republic.

Long live France.
