My dear fellow citizens,

For the sixth time, I have the privilege of sharing this moment of New Year’s wishes with you.

With you, the officers and soldiers of our armed forces who protect us; with you, police officers, gendarmes, firefighters, security forces and sea rescuers who are keeping watch this evening. With you, doctors, healthcare workers and patients. With you, public servants who ensure the continuity of our public services. With you, citizens engaged in their professional lives or in our associations. With you, our fellow citizens overseas. With all of you who are with your families in France or abroad, and more particularly with those of you whose circumstances in life mean that you feel alone this evening. Those of you who are going through hardship, and to whom our thoughts go.

New Year’s addresses have something unique about them: they compel us to speak of a future which, in truth, we do not know, but which we know with certainty we will have to face, with our strengths and our weaknesses, but as a united country.

In carrying out the responsibilities entrusted to me, I never lose sight of this imperative of the unity of the nation that we all form together.

If we were to give in to the spirit of division that presses upon us from every side, we would have virtually no chance of overcoming the challenges before us in such a harsh world and in such difficult times. And so, for 2023, I wish for us, as far as possible, to live as a united and supportive country, recognizing the place of each person and respecting everyone.

I think back to the wishes I expressed to you at the same time one year ago. Who could have imagined then that, just as we thought we were emerging, with great difficulty, from a global pandemic, within a matter of weeks we would have to confront unimaginable challenges: war returning to European soil following Russia’s aggression against Ukraine and its democracy; tens, perhaps hundreds, of thousands of deaths; millions of refugees; a terrible energy crisis; a looming food crisis; and the invocation of the gravest threats, including nuclear ones? Who could have predicted the wave of inflation that followed? Or the climate crisis, whose dramatic effects were once again visible in our country this summer?

And yet, throughout these seasons of danger, what has remained constant, once again this year, is our collective ability to rise together to these challenges.

Yes, during this year, France, together with Europe, has upheld the voice of law and freedom in support of Ukraine; thousands of you demonstrated solidarity by welcoming into our towns and villages refugees fleeing the Russian invasion.

National solidarity, financed by French taxpayers, has made it possible to mitigate the increase in energy prices for everyone, safeguard our businesses, and particularly protect the incomes of the most modest among us. And thanks to our collective action, we supported growth, kept inflation at levels lower than those experienced by our neighbours, and brought unemployment down to its lowest level in fifteen years. Indeed, this year we continued to create jobs, and quality jobs.

With every challenge, Europe has enabled us to act faster and more forcefully: to build a united front of resistance against Russia, to reinvest in our industries, and above all to find, on the scale of the continent, responses to the challenges of our century. Thus, during the first six months of this year, when France held the Presidency of the Council of the European Union, we took decisions to reduce our greenhouse gas emissions by more than half by 2030; we agreed on a carbon tax at our borders to protect our industries and our planet; together, we decided to establish a minimum tax on large multinational corporations in order to combat tax evasion; and together, we began to regulate major digital platforms more effectively.

And despite all these crises and all these challenges, 2022 was also, for all of us, a year of intense democratic activity, during which you renewed our National Assembly and, in the presidential election this spring, decided to entrust me with a new five-year mandate at the head of our nation, an honour that also places obligations upon me.

2022 was also, once again, a year in which our country shone in the arts, culture and sport, with two Nobel Prizes and so many great moments of creativity and sporting achievement. Let us be proud of all this, and of everything we have accomplished together during the year that is now ending.

*

We must carry this pride and this confidence with us as we enter the year ahead. For I believe that 2023 will first of all be a year of questions that I know are causing concern, and of crises that we will once again have to confront.

Will we face electricity cuts? If we continue to save energy, as we have been doing for the past few months and as I announced to you as early as 14 July, and if we continue to bring our nuclear reactors back into service as planned, we will get through it. It is in our hands.

Will energy prices rise? Here too, we have put concrete responses in place. I say this to each and every one of you because, although energy prices have reached historic levels, increases will remain capped in our country. I say this in particular to our tradespeople, our bakers, but also to our most industrial businesses. Starting tomorrow, you will have tailored support in addition to the tariff shield already in place, so that the long-term survival of your businesses, our jobs and our competitiveness can be ensured. And whenever necessary, the Government will adapt its responses, as it has done at every stage. Above all, we will accelerate solutions that will enable us to free ourselves from our dependencies and establish an electricity price that reflects its actual cost of production.

Will we once again experience a wave of Covid, and will the epidemic ever end? Here too, we will be able to cope. First, thanks to the reasonable and appropriate use of the protective measures against the virus that we have learned. Second, thanks to vaccination, which has demonstrated its effectiveness; and this evening I particularly encourage all our fellow citizens over the age of 70 to receive their booster dose, or to get vaccinated if they have not already done so. Finally, by testing, sequencing and introducing border controls.

That is what the Government has decided to do in our airports starting tomorrow for flights arriving from China, where the epidemic is in full swing and where many restrictions have been lifted.

Will we have to work longer in 2023? Here too, as I pledged before you, this year will indeed be the year of a pension reform intended to ensure the balance of our system for the years and decades ahead. We need to work more. That is the very purpose of the unemployment insurance reform introduced by the Government and passed by Parliament. It is also the purpose of this reform, on which the social partners and the Government will work in the coming months in order to finalize the new rules that will apply from the end of the summer of 2023.

The objective is to strengthen our pay-as-you-go pension schemes, which without such measures would be threatened, because we continue to finance them through borrowing. To achieve this, the extension of our working lives will be gradual and will take place in stages over nearly ten years. It will also be fair, taking account of long careers, interrupted careers, and the difficulty of certain tasks and professions.

But this is what will enable us to balance the financing of our pensions in our country and, in a country where people are living longer—which is a fortunate thing—to improve the minimum pension for all those who have worked the required number of quarters, and to pass on to our children a social model that is fair and strong because it will be credible and sustainably financed.

Throughout the long history of our nation, there have been generations called upon to resist, others called upon to rebuild, and still others called upon to expand the prosperity that had been achieved.

As for us, it falls to us to confront this new chapter in a difficult era and, beyond the emergencies of this year, some of which I have just mentioned, to take responsibility for rebuilding many of the pillars of our nation, whether our schools, our healthcare system, our transport, the development of our regions, our industries, and many others.

To do this, we must remain united, but we must also preserve an undiminished collective ambition: an ambition to continue transforming our country in the face of vested interests, in the face of all the apparently good reasons for continuing as before, and in the face of the temptation to succumb to a spirit of defeat.

That is how we will serve our young people and the generations to come. Over the past five years, we have already done much of this work of transformation, simplification and overcoming inertia. But it is sometimes still too little, and too often too slow.

And like you, I too grow impatient, but without ever giving in to easy solutions or fatalism. And so my wish for us in 2023 is that, through our work and our commitment, we strive to rebuild a France that is stronger and fairer, so that we may pass it on to our children.

It is through our work and our commitment that we will be able to increase the resources of our domestic security forces, our justice system and our armed forces in the face of new geopolitical challenges and the multiplication of conflicts.

It is through our work and our commitment that we will fight more effectively against trafficking and against illegal immigration, both in mainland France and in our overseas territories. Let us always remain faithful to our values. Let us integrate people more effectively through language and work; let us protect the women and men who fight for freedom, such as those coming from Iran or elsewhere; but let us maintain control of our borders and preserve the unity of the nation.

It is through our work and our commitment that we will also build a productive and environmentally responsible nation. Because the ecological transition is a battle that we must win, we must pursue it with determination and method. Ecological planning will be the instrument of this historic transformation, enabling us to reduce our CO2 emissions and preserve our biodiversity. With our France 2030 plan, we will continue to invest, innovate and deploy environmental solutions on an industrial scale. And following the law intended to accelerate the development of renewable energy, the nuclear energy law will mark the beginning of the construction of new power plants on our territory.

It is through our work and our commitment that we will also restore a nation founded on trust. By making our secularism effective every day, because it is a principle of freedom. And it takes nothing away from the place of religion in each person’s private conscience, for all those who believe. At this moment, I pay tribute to the memory of His Holiness Benedict XVI, whose passing, I know, affects many Catholics in France and throughout the world.

This nation of trust will also be strengthened by an unwavering fight against all forms of discrimination, within a society founded on respect and recognition. It will also be reinforced by the daily struggle that we will continue to pursue in support of people with disabilities.

It is through our work and our commitment that we must rebuild our great public services so that they fully uphold our ideal of equality. Through the National Council for Refoundation, we have begun to renew the contract between generations and to work towards providing better support for our elderly citizens who are losing their independence.

We have also begun to restore confidence in our national education system and our healthcare system, drawing on the energy and dedication of our teachers and healthcare workers. We will pursue this work vigorously throughout the year ahead, through clear and strong choices and through action carried out as close as possible to the realities on the ground.

It is through our work and our commitment that we will build a fairer society. A fairer society is first and foremost one in which equality between women and men is a reality. This, which has been the great cause of both of my presidential terms, will continue to shape the Government’s work and inspire our actions every day.

But a fairer society is also one that is fairer socially. Not through higher taxes—no. Nor by leaving more debt to future generations. But by improving the support we provide to our children and adolescents, reforming vocational secondary education, and improving the guidance provided to our young people so that they can find the right training and the right careers.

By reindustrializing our country faster and more strongly, we will create new jobs and careers for the future. For the greatest injustice in our country remains the influence of family background and the weakness of social mobility. And the answer lies in schools, in career guidance, in higher education, in our innovation policy and in our industrialization.

And because confidence in the vitality of our democratic life has also weakened, we know that we will have much to do in the months ahead. In the coming weeks and months, I will lay the first foundations of a Universal National Service. We will have to initiate the necessary changes to our institutions and to our public and civic life. And together with the National Assembly and the Senate, as well as the Economic, Social and Environmental Council, we will have to build a better functioning of our institutions and involve our citizens more frequently.

France and Europe have an eminent role to play at this moment in the history of humanity, as so many authoritarian regimes challenge our democracies and their foundations. Protecting our children, protecting our free and independent information, protecting the free and just order that enables citizens to live happy lives: these are some of the struggles of our time.

We have a duty to take them on.

Yes, it is through our work and our commitment that we will give ourselves the means to fulfil our aspirations for today and our ambitions for tomorrow.

In short, in 2023, step by step, we will have to consolidate our energy, economic, social, industrial, financial and strategic independence, and strengthen what I might call our strength of spirit. This is what we owe to our children.

This is not, and never will be, a pursuit of illusions doomed from the outset. For French independence must find in Europe its extension and its complement. At a time when so many countries dream of becoming empires, and so many companies imagine themselves as kingdoms, we have a continent capable of forming an area of peace and freedom, prosperity, solidarity, law and power. Since 2017, we have pursued this struggle to bring Europe’s strengths together. So that, as 27 nations, we may be stronger.

That is why I have fought this battle in recent years, why you have heard me warn other Europeans as well about the need to defend our industries and our jobs following decisions taken on other continents a few weeks ago, and why I will redouble my efforts at the beginning of this year.

*

My dear fellow citizens,

Here and this evening, on your behalf, I want to say to our Ukrainian friends: we respect you and we admire you. Your struggle in defence of your nation is heroic, and it inspires us. And throughout the year ahead, we will stand unwaveringly by your side. We will help you until victory, and together we will work to build a just and lasting peace. You can count on France, and you can count on Europe.

As for us, let us be this generation of builders.

In the coming months of 2023, the reconstruction work on Notre-Dame de Paris, this extraordinary project, will move closer to completion. In the coming months, our entire country will come together in a spirit of fraternity as we approach the Olympic and Paralympic Games and as France hosts the Rugby World Cup. In the coming months, thanks to the France 2030 plan, the first electric cars built entirely on our territory will roll out of our factories.

And so many other projects will take shape throughout our country, enabling France both to reduce carbon emissions and to reduce unemployment. In the coming months, in our classrooms, in our hospitals and in our doctors’ practices, you will see the first tangible changes resulting from the renewal of our schools and our healthcare system.

For all of this, I wish for all of us unity, boldness, collective ambition and kindness. We have overcome so many crises together, my dear fellow citizens. I know that we are capable of overcoming those that lie ahead and of being the generation entrusted with the responsibility of rebuilding France and Europe so that we may pass them on to our children stronger, more beautiful and more just.

Together, we will succeed.

And it is with this confidence that I extend to all of you my very best wishes for the New Year.

Long live the Republic.

Long live France.
